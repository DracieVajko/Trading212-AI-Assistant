# Market Evaluation Playbook

Living checklist distilled from external morning briefings and catalyst
research. It records **how to evaluate** markets, not day-specific calls.
Dated brief summaries live in `briefs/`; rules here must stay general enough
to survive the next week.

## 1. Catalyst quality screen

A trade idea earns attention only when all four are true:

1. **Confirmed event or fresh reaction** — a scheduled event (earnings, IPO,
   central-bank decision) or a measurable price/sector move already underway.
2. **Multi-session transmission mechanism** — a plausible path from the event
   to revenue, margins, capex or positioning over 1–3 weeks. A one-day
   headline is not a thesis.
3. **Listed, tradable exposure** — at least one instrument with a clear
   economic link, checked for exchange, liquidity, spread and suitability.
4. **Explicit invalidation** — the observable outcome that proves the idea
   wrong. No invalidation, no trade.

Rank ideas by **persistence of attention**, not by expected return. Labels
(high / medium) are editorial, never probabilities.

## 2. Event persistence test

- Require **2–3 sessions** of confirmation: improving relative strength,
  volume expansion, holding above the breakout/support level.
- A single relief bounce after a negative event is noise.
- Compare the traded name against its sector and the broad index (relative
  strength), not only its own price.
- If the event was two-sided (e.g. earnings), wait for the **reaction**, not
  the headline.

## 3. Earnings and guidance

- Strong demand ≠ strong stock. A beat can still be sold: the market reacts
  to guidance, margins, capex and positioning.
- Post-earnings price action is the signal: a peer that beat and still fell
  for several sessions warns that the sector's expectations are too high.
- Quality test: contract/bookings growth vs sustainable free-cash-flow
  conversion; ARR/RPO quality vs one-off licence revenue; order backlog vs
  actual delivery and cash collection.

## 4. Leading indicators to monitor

- **Capex revisions** (AI infrastructure, hyperscalers): a cut to multi-year
  capex forecasts is a leading negative for the supply chain even while
  current revenue is strong.
- **Memory/HBM demand and pricing**; foundry utilisation; networking/ASIC
  orders.
- **Relative strength**: SOX vs S&P 500, sector index vs STOXX 600, equal-
  weight vs cap-weight breadth.
- **Rates and discount rates**: PPI/CPI, Treasury yields, central-bank
  expectations. Rising real yields compress long-duration growth valuations.
- **Macro demand**: GDP/PMI, retail sales, industrial production, China data.
- **Positioning/rotation**: money leaving crowded themes (AI) into defence,
  industrials, healthcare, value retail.

## 5. What "good" and "bad" look like

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Confirmed catalyst with a dated event | Rumour or one-off headline |
| Multi-session relative strength + volume | One-day spike on thin volume |
| Guidance/margins improving | Beat with weak guidance or margin compression |
| Capex/backlog converting to cash | Capex cuts, backlog without delivery |
| Clear invalidation level | "It can only go up" thesis |
| Diversified, sized position | Concentrated bet on one theme |
| Stop defined before entry | Averaging down into a broken thesis |

## 6. Risk and sizing rules

- Define the stop and the invalidation **before** entry; size from the stop,
  not from conviction.
- Cap single-theme concentration; do not over-bet one supply chain.
- In a de-rating sector, reduce exposure or sell into minor rebounds rather
  than waiting for the old high.
- Do not hunt the absolute bottom; wait for stabilisation (higher low,
  volume, relative strength) before re-entry.
- A stopped-out trade is data: score it, record why, and require fresh
  confirmation before re-entering the same symbol.

## 7. Information evaluation

- Prefer primary sources (company IR, exchange filings, official statistics)
  over summaries; note the publication date and whether the event is
  **announced** or **already reported**.
- Separate three claims: (a) what happened, (b) what it means mechanically,
  (c) what the price may do. Only (a) and (b) are evidence; (c) is a
  hypothesis.
- Treat "smart money", "institutions are buying" and similar labels as
  narrative unless backed by observable flow or filings.
- Verify numbers against a second source; avoid acting on a single headline.
- Record the invalidation and the confirmation checklist for every idea, so
  the next review can grade the process rather than the outcome.

## 8. Brief ingestion template

When a new external brief arrives, add a dated summary under `briefs/` with:

- date and source;
- 3–6 key facts (numbers, dates, events);
- what confirmed / invalidated since the previous brief;
- which playbook rules were exercised;
- open questions to verify at the next run.

Promote a rule into this playbook only when it is general, observable and
testable. Day-specific calls stay in the dated brief summary.
